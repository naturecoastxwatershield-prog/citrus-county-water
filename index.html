2024 Homosassa SpringsWatch

The Homosassa Springs Group is a first-magnitude spring in Ellie Schiller Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park. The Homosassa River flows about six miles west to the Gulf of Mexico, and the river system is tidally influenced.

  • Monitoring period: January–December 2024
  • Monitoring stations: Fish Bowl (HOM-1) and Main Bridge (HOM-2)
  • Water temperature: approximately 24°C (75.2°F) on average at the spring system
  • Average horizontal Secchi visibility: 9.6 m (31.5 ft)
  • Fish observations: 14 species recorded across nine monitoring sessions

A number that deserves attention

2.12 mg/L NOx-N

The September 2024 nitrate+nitrite sample at the Fish Bowl was reported as 2.12 mg/L. The report compares this with the 0.35 mg/L springs impairment level cited from the Florida Department of Environmental Protection—approximately 6.1 times that level.

This was a single September sample at HOM-1, so it should be presented with the report's stated sampling context rather than as a year-round average.

Water clarity & light

The report explains that higher diffuse attenuation means light is lost more rapidly through the water. At the Fish Bowl, the average attenuation coefficient (k) was 1.4; at Main Bridge, the recorded value was 0.5, although only one MB measurement was available for that comparison.

Fish community

Among the fish recorded, minnows were the most frequently observed category, followed by striped mullet. The report emphasizes that fish support food webs, nutrient cycling, and the recreational and tourism value associated with Florida's springs.

KBAM Phytoremediation Demonstration Project

The KBAM project examined the feasibility of using floating aquatic plants—particularly water hyacinths—within a protected treatment area in Kings Bay, while using manatee-exclusion cages to allow the plants to establish and survive grazing.

  • The project began in 2012 as a multi-year demonstration.
  • Floating PVC structures and manatee-exclusion cages were developed and deployed.
  • Water quality, light attenuation, water clarity, plants, and aquatic fauna were monitored.
  • The report documents ecological observations and the practical challenges of establishing floating plants in a manatee-inhabited spring system.
